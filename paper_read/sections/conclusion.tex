\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Small local LLMs cannot triage alerts on their own, but they can be made useful for alert types that analysts already know. \hesp{} divides triage: the model reads each probe's log text into an outcome analysts defined, and a controller chooses the probes, decides when to stop, and accepts only verdicts that ledger evidence supports. On three simulated settings, neither half works alone. Deciding from raw logs, a 7B model resolves none of 48 cases, and the controller's rule parser fails completely once the log format changes, whereas with Qwen2.5-7B reading for the controller all 48 are resolved. Reading is also where attackers write, so \hesp{} never lets the model's own reading support a benign verdict; with this rule, the finish guard, and source-diverse corroboration, no injected instruction, forged source, or adaptive attack on the reader closes a case. Two questions remain before deployment: whether tables counted from real resolved tickets, which are less extreme than ours, still let the controller settle a case in a few probes, and whether a model can help where the candidate causes are not known in advance.

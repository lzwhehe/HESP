\begin{abstract}
Organizations that keep telemetry in-house must triage alerts with small local LLMs. Triage involves two jobs: \emph{reading} probe results into evidence, and \emph{deciding} which probe to run next, when to stop, and which verdict to accept. We show that small models fail at deciding but are needed for reading, and present \hesp{}, which lets the model read while a controller decides. The controller keeps a Bayesian ledger over a given list of candidate causes, selects read-only probes by expected information gain, and accepts only verdicts backed by ledger evidence. In three simulated triage settings with five open-weight models from 7B to 72B, small models deciding on their own resolve 0 to 13\% of cases, whereas the controller resolves every case in two settings with no model at all, and no model improves on it: deciding needs no model. When a logging change alters the log format, a fixed parser reads none of 364 observations and every case escalates, while Qwen2.5-7B as reader resolves all 48 episodes, as many as with structured observations: reading needs the model. Reading is also an attack channel: an attacker who writes a consistent benign story into every log, in a format whose genuine records do not contradict it, makes Qwen2.5-7B as reader close all 36 attacks as benign. Never letting model-parsed evidence support a benign verdict closes every attack we tested, at the cost of escalating honest benign cases when no trusted parser can read the logs. Our settings assume that the candidate causes are known in advance and that each cause leaves a distinctive observation; open-world triage is outside their scope.
\end{abstract}

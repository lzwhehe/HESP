\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLM agents in security operations} Recent work evaluates and builds LLM agents for incident analysis and triage. SIABench benchmarks LLM agents on security incident analysis and alert triage~\citep{siabench}, ExCyTIn-Bench on threat investigation over incident graphs~\citep{excytin}, and CORTEX coordinates several LLM agents for alert triage~\citep{cortex}; Clouseau reconstructs attack narratives with a hierarchy of LLM agents and reports that open-weight models trail proprietary ones~\citep{clouseau}. These works measure or improve how well a model investigates; we ask what an attacker can do once the model investigates, and move the decisions out of it. Closest in spirit, SENTINEL-RL restricts the LLM to queries and summaries and hands containment decisions to a trained policy~\citep{sentinelrl}; we make the same division for triage verdicts and evaluate it against attackers. LogInject measures passive prompt injection through log fields against LLM log analysis and finds that no single defense removes it~\citep{loginject}; our reader attacks confirm the threat in a triage agent and show which architectural rule stops it.

\paragraph{Prompt-injection defenses} Model-level defenses separate instructions from data in the input format or in training~\citep{struq,secalign}, and detection-style defenses such as MELON~\citep{melon} compare the agent's behavior with and without the retrieved data; none gives a guarantee against an adaptive attacker~\citep{promptinjection_bench,agentdojo}. System-level defenses constrain what the agent may do: IsolateGPT isolates LLM apps~\citep{isolategpt}, Progent enforces privilege policies on tool calls~\citep{progent}, and CaMeL and FIDES track the provenance of values and enforce policies before tools run~\citep{camel,fides}. \hesp{} shares their premise that security must not depend on the model's reasoning, but a triage agent has no dangerous tool: its only harmful output is the verdict. Reader trust is a provenance rule of the same kind, applied to the one decision that matters in triage and made asymmetric in the verdict.

\paragraph{Provenance-based detection and triage} NoDoze ranks alerts by the anomaly of their surrounding provenance~\citep{nodoze}, and Kairos detects and reconstructs intrusions from whole-system provenance~\citep{kairos}. They decide what an analyst should look at; \hesp{} decides which query runs next and whether the evidence suffices.

\paragraph{Sequential diagnosis} Choosing tests by expected information gain goes back to Lindley~\citep{lindley1956} and Bayesian experimental design~\citep{chaloner1995}, underlies model-based diagnosis~\citep{dekleer1987}, and has near-optimality guarantees for noisy hypothesis identification~\citep{dasgupta2004,golovin2010}. We use the criterion unchanged; our contribution is where it sits relative to the model and what that does to the attacker.

Organizations that keep telemetry in-house must triage alerts with small local LLMs, which cannot do it on their own. Many alerts, however, belong to recurring types whose candidate causes, distinguishing checks, and past resolutions analysts already know. We present HESP, which makes a small model usable for such alert types by dividing triage: the model reads each probe's raw log text into an outcome analysts defined, and a controller decides which probe to run, when to stop, and which verdict to accept, using a Bayesian ledger with likelihoods counted from resolved cases. In three simulated settings with five open-weight models (7B to 72B), small models deciding alone resolve at most a third of cases with labeled observations and none of 48 when they must read raw logs. The controller alone resolves every case in two settings when observations are structured, but after a log-format change its rule parser reads none of 364 observations. Together, with Qwen2.5-7B reading and the controller deciding, all 48 drifted-format episodes are resolved. Reading is also an attack channel: a consistent benign story written into every log makes Qwen2.5-7B close all 36 attacks as benign; never letting model-parsed evidence support a benign verdict stops every attack we tested, at the cost of escalating honest benign cases when no trusted parser can read the logs. Our settings are simulated, their causes and outcomes are written in advance, and each cause leaves a distinctive observation. Code, protocols, and episode journals: https://github.com/lzwhehe/HESP

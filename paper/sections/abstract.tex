\begin{abstract}
Organizations that cannot send telemetry to hosted models must automate alert triage with small open-weight LLMs, and these models fail at the investigation procedure: they probe without converging, do not commit to a verdict, or follow instructions written into the logs. We ask which triage decisions should be taken from the model, and what security boundary results. \hesp{} is a controller that keeps a ledger of candidate causes, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepts only verdicts backed by current evidence, and can end an investigation itself. In a controlled study on two synthetic task families with predefined causes and discrete probe outcomes (six studies with internally frozen protocols, five models from 7B to 72B, 19{,}838 audited LLM episodes), choosing what to probe and deciding when to stop are separate failures that differ between models. Once the controller applies the verifier's evidence standard, it completes every case without an LLM, and a pre-registered comparison finds no gain from letting Qwen2.5-72B decide when to stop. The finish guard blocks injected instructions to dismiss an alert on every model, whereas a forged source defeats the controller unless benign verdicts require corroboration from distinct source groups, which costs half of the honest benign cases. Real-log parsing, where the model may still be needed, remains untested.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
LLM agents promise to automate alert triage, and organizations that must keep telemetry on their premises can deploy only small open-weight models. These agents read logs that the attacker partly writes, and the attacker needs little: an alert closed as benign. Current LLM investigation agents leave the whole procedure to the model, from choosing queries and reading their results to deciding when the evidence suffices, so text placed in a log field can end an investigation. Defenses for tool-using agents restrict what an agent may do; a triage agent, however, only reads, and its harmful output is a verdict. To remedy this situation, the decisions of an investigation must be taken from the model, and every remaining path from attacker-controlled data to a verdict must be closed. In this paper, we present \hesp{}, a controller that holds alert triage outside a local LLM. \hesp{} keeps a Bayesian ledger of candidate causes, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, and accepts a verdict only if a current observation in the ledger supports it, such that instructions written into logs cannot close a case. For the paths that remain, we devise source-group corroboration of benign verdicts against forged sources, and a reader-trust rule under which log readings parsed by the model may confirm an attack but never a benign verdict. In a controlled study with five open-weight models from 7B to 72B on three synthetic triage families, \hesp{} blocks injected dismissals on every model and lets no forged-source or adaptive log-injection attack close a case, whereas without these rules up to every attack is closed as benign. The controller alone completes every case of the two deterministic families, so the model is left only to read.
\end{abstract}

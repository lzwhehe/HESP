\begin{abstract}
We conduct a security study of alert triage by small, locally run LLM agents, which organizations adopt when their security logs cannot leave the premises, and which read logs that an attacker partly writes. Our study is made possible through \hesp{}, a controller that can take each triage decision away from the model, and a testbed of three simulated triage settings with attackers who write into logs, forge data sources, and target the model while it parses raw logs. Leveraging them with five open-weight models from 7B to 72B, we have observed that every model that decides on its own follows an instruction planted in a log to close the alert, whereas a controller that accepts only verdicts backed by recorded evidence blocks it on every model. Furthermore, once the controller decides, a single forged source closes every attack as benign, unless a benign verdict needs support from two independent sources. When the model must read raw logs, an attacker who writes into every log closes up to every attack, and a rule that never lets the model's own readings support a benign verdict closes none. Also observed is that taking the decisions from the model costs no accuracy: in two of the three settings, the controller alone resolves every case.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Security teams that cannot send telemetry to a hosted model must triage alerts with small local LLMs. We find that such agents fail on procedure: they repeat queries, stop before the decisive evidence, or never commit to a verdict. We present \hesp{}, a controller between the model and its read-only investigation tools. \hesp{} keeps a ledger of competing explanations, selects probes by expected information gain per unit cost, accepts a verdict only when current evidence supports it, and journals every prediction before the observation it predicts.

In a controlled triage environment with eight hidden causes, we ran four pre-registered studies with five open-weight models from two families (7B to 72B), 7{,}272 audited episodes in total. With probe-outcome tables counted from LLM-free development episodes, \hesp{} lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion, matching oracle tables. A control that holds the planner's information fixed attributes $+0.26$ to $+0.35$ of completion to the information-gain ranking itself. Taking probe choice away from the model helps 7B models and hurts 32B and 72B ones. Llama-3.1-8B never concludes on its own. A controller-side stopping rule lifts it from 0 to 0.917, after which controller probe selection adds no detectable completion. A post hoc run of the same controller with no LLM also reaches 0.917, at the lowest probe cost of any configuration. The best LLM configurations reach 1.000 because strong models collect confirming evidence before they finish, whereas the controller concludes by elimination. Once observations arrive as discrete outcomes, the investigation procedure carries most of triage. We also show why three public security datasets cannot test investigation policies.
\end{abstract}

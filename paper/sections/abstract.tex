\begin{abstract}
Security operations centers receive far more alerts than analysts can investigate, and organizations that cannot send their telemetry to hosted models must automate triage with small open-weight LLMs on their own hardware. Current LLM agents leave the investigation procedure to the model, and small local models fail at it: they probe without converging, never commit to a verdict, or follow instructions an attacker writes into the logs. In this paper, we present \hesp{}, a controller that holds the investigation procedure outside the model. \hesp{} keeps a ledger of competing explanations, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepts only verdicts backed by current evidence, can end an investigation itself, and journals every prediction before its observation. We evaluated \hesp{} in five pre-registered studies with five open-weight models from two families (7B to 72B), totalling 11{,}912 audited episodes. With likelihood tables counted from LLM-free runs, \hesp{} lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion, and a controller-side stop lifts Llama-3.1-8B, which never concludes on its own, from 0 to 0.917: what to probe and when to stop are separate failures. On a second task family built from public Sigma detection rules, the information-gain ranking again raises verified completion by 0.11 to 0.23 on every model. When the logs carry an instruction to dismiss the alert, every LLM that decides its own verdict complies, while \hesp{} never does. A single forged observation, however, fools the controller as well, and a rule that requires two independent confirmations for benign verdicts turns these cases into escalations. We release all code, protocols, and episode journals.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Organizations that cannot send telemetry to hosted models must triage security alerts with small local LLMs, and such agents fail on procedure: they keep probing without concluding, or never commit to a verdict. We argue that the investigation procedure belongs outside the model. \hesp{} is a controller that keeps a ledger of competing explanations, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepts only evidence-backed verdicts, can end the investigation itself, and journals every prediction before its observation. In four pre-registered studies with five open-weight models (7B to 72B, two families; 7{,}272 audited episodes) in a controlled triage environment, probe-outcome tables counted from LLM-free runs lift Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion, matching oracle tables. The information-gain ranking adds $+0.26$ to $+0.35$ on every model that concludes, and a controller-side stop lifts Llama-3.1-8B, which never concludes on its own, from 0 to 0.917. What to probe and when to stop are separate failures, and different small models exhibit different ones. The controller alone reaches 0.917, while strong models deciding when to stop reach 1.000 by waiting for confirming evidence. We also show why three public SOC datasets cannot test investigation policies, and we release all code, protocols, and episode journals.
\end{abstract}

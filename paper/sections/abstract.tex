Organizations that keep telemetry on their premises can automate alert triage only with small open-weight LLM agents. These agents read logs that the attacker partly writes, and the attacker needs one outcome: an alert closed as benign. Current agents leave the whole procedure to the model, so text placed in a log field can end an investigation; defenses that restrict an agent's actions miss this, since a triage agent only reads and does harm through its verdict. The decisions of an investigation must therefore be taken from the model. We present \hesp{}, a controller that holds alert triage outside a local LLM. \hesp{} keeps a Bayesian ledger of candidate causes, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, and accepts a verdict only if current evidence in the ledger supports it, such that instructions written into logs cannot close a case. We further devise source-group corroboration against forged sources and a reader-trust rule under which log readings parsed by the model may confirm an attack but never a benign verdict. Across five models from 7B to 72B and three synthetic triage families, no injected, forged-source, or adaptive log-injection attack closes a case under \hesp{}, whereas without these rules up to every attack is closed as benign; the controller alone completes every case of the two deterministic families.

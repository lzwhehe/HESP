\begin{abstract}
LLM agents promise to automate alert triage, and organizations that keep telemetry on their premises can deploy only small open-weight models. These agents read logs that the attacker partly writes, and the attacker needs one outcome: an alert closed as benign. Current investigation agents leave the whole procedure to the model, so text placed in a log field can end an investigation; defenses that restrict an agent's actions do not address this, since a triage agent only reads and its harmful output is a verdict. To remedy this situation, the decisions of an investigation must be taken from the model. In this paper, we present \hesp{}, a controller that holds alert triage outside a local LLM. \hesp{} keeps a Bayesian ledger of candidate causes, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, and accepts a verdict only if current evidence in the ledger supports it, such that instructions written into logs cannot close a case. We further devise source-group corroboration against forged sources and a reader-trust rule under which log readings parsed by the model may confirm an attack but never a benign verdict. Across five open-weight models from 7B to 72B and three synthetic triage families, no injected, forged-source, or adaptive log-injection attack closes a case under \hesp{}, whereas without these rules up to every attack is closed as benign; the controller alone completes every case of the two deterministic families.
\end{abstract}

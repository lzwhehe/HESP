\begin{abstract}
Security operations centers receive far more alerts than analysts can investigate, and organizations that cannot send their telemetry to hosted models must automate triage with small open-weight LLMs on their own hardware. Current LLM agents leave the investigation procedure to the model, and small local models fail at it: they probe without converging, do not commit to a verdict, or follow instructions an attacker writes into the logs. We present \hesp{}, a controller that holds the investigation procedure outside the model: it keeps a ledger of competing explanations, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepts only verdicts backed by current evidence, can end an investigation itself, and journals every prediction before its observation. We evaluate \hesp{} as a controlled mechanism study in two predominantly deterministic synthetic task families with predefined hypotheses and discrete probe outcomes, in six pre-registered studies with five open-weight models (7B to 72B) and 19{,}838 audited LLM episodes. Tables estimated from development rollouts match generator-derived tables, without direct access to the generator probabilities. Choosing what to probe and deciding when to stop are separate failures: with the finish guard, controller selection lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion, and in a separate study a controller-side stop lifts Llama-3.1-8B, which did not conclude under our prompt, from 0 to 0.917. The information-gain ranking replicates on a second family whose causes come from public Sigma rules. Given the verifier's evidence standard, the controller alone completes every case in both families, and no model adds to it: the pre-registered comparison with Qwen2.5-72B deciding when to stop finds no gain ($+0.000$ and $-0.028$). Under the four injection texts we evaluated, guarded \hesp{} closed no attack as benign on any model, whereas every model that decided and reached its own verdict followed the injected instruction; the finish guard is the component that provides this. A forged observation fools the controller too, and requiring corroboration from distinct source groups for benign verdicts turns these cases into escalations at a cost on benign alerts. Real-log parsing and open-world hypotheses remain untested.
\end{abstract}

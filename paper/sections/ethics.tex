\section*{Ethics Considerations}
This work involves no human subjects and no live systems. All episodes run against loopback sandboxes we built, the agent issues only read-only queries, and the adversarial variants simulate an attacker inside those sandboxes; no exploit or attack tooling was written or run. Public datasets are used within their licences (Appendix~\ref{app:licences}), the GUIDE test split was never read (the ExCyTIn-Bench feasibility checks did read its test questions; erratum E-10), and no per-incident GUIDE data is redistributed.

\paragraph{Potential harms of publication} The adversarial results describe how an attacker could mislead an automated triage agent: by writing instructions into log fields, or by making one source give a benign-looking answer. Both techniques are known in general form, and we publish them with measured mitigations, deciding verdicts from structured evidence against the first and requiring independent corroboration for benign verdicts against the single-source form of the second. We consider deploying triage agents without knowing these failure modes more harmful than reporting them. We release the controller, environments, and journals, but no attack tooling.

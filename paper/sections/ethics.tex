\section*{Ethics Considerations}
This work involves no human subjects and no live systems: all episodes run against loopback sandboxes we built, with read-only queries and a simulated attacker; no exploit or attack tooling was written or run. Public datasets are used within their licences (Appendix~\ref{app:licences}), the GUIDE test split was never read (the ExCyTIn-Bench feasibility checks did read its test questions; erratum E-10), and no per-incident GUIDE data is redistributed.

\paragraph{Potential harms of publication} The adversarial results describe how an attacker could mislead an automated triage agent: by writing instructions into log fields, or by making one source give a benign-looking answer. Both techniques are known in general form; we publish them with measured mitigations (the finish guard, and source-group corroboration for benign verdicts), since deploying triage agents without knowing these failure modes is the greater harm. We release the controller, environments, and journals, but no attack tooling.

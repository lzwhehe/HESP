\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we introduced \hesp{}, a controller that holds the alert-triage procedure outside a small local LLM. \hesp{} splits an investigation into what to probe next and when the evidence suffices: a hypothesis ledger with counted likelihood tables ranks read-only probes by information gain per cost, an evidence rule and a controller-side stop govern verdicts, and a write-ahead journal makes every step auditable. Five pre-registered studies with five open-weight models and 11{,}912 episodes show that \hesp{} lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion without oracle knowledge, that its ranking helps every model on our own environment and on a family derived from public Sigma rules, and that choosing probes and deciding to stop are separate failures that different small models exhibit differently. The controller also contains instructions injected into log content, which every model that decided its own verdict followed; a forged observation, in contrast, defeats it unless benign verdicts require independent corroboration. Testing the model's remaining role, reading raw telemetry, requires paired data that public security datasets lack.

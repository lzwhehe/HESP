Many security teams cannot send telemetry to hosted LLMs and must run small local models for alert triage. We find that such models often fail for reasons unrelated to security knowledge: they repeat queries, stop too early, or never commit to a verdict. We present HESP, a controller that sits between the model and its read-only investigation tools. HESP keeps a ledger of competing explanations, chooses probes by expected information gain per unit cost, and accepts only verdicts backed by current evidence. In a controlled triage environment with eight hidden causes, we ran four pre-registered studies with five open-weight models (7B to 72B, two families), more than 7,000 audited episodes in total. With prediction tables estimated only from LLM-free development episodes, HESP lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion, the same as with oracle tables. A randomized control that holds the planner's information fixed attributes +0.26 to +0.35 of completion to the information-gain ranking itself. Taking probe choice away from the model helps a 7B model but hurts 32B and 72B models. Llama-3.1-8B never concludes in any configuration. A controller-side stopping rule lifts it from 0 to 0.917, but once stopping is supplied, controller probe selection no longer beats the model's own choices: what this model lacked was when to stop, not what to probe. Finally, we show why three public security datasets (ExCyTIn-Bench, GUIDE, OTRF) cannot test investigation policies, and we release code, frozen protocols, errata, and episode journals.

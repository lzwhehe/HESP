Security operations centers receive far more alerts than analysts can investigate, and organizations that cannot send their telemetry to hosted models must automate triage with small open-weight LLMs on their own hardware. Left to run an investigation themselves, small local models fail at the procedure: they probe without converging, never commit to a verdict, or dismiss real attacks. We study what happens when the procedure is moved out of the model. HESP is a controller that keeps a ledger of competing explanations, selects read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepts only verdicts backed by current evidence, can end an investigation itself, and journals every prediction before its observation. In four pre-registered studies with five open-weight models from two families (7B to 72B), totalling 7,272 audited episodes in a controlled triage environment that we built, moving the procedure out of the model lifts Qwen2.5-7B from 0.125 to 1.000 verified completion and Llama-3.1-8B, which never concludes on its own, from 0 to 0.917; the information-gain ranking adds +0.26 to +0.35 on every model that concludes. The controller alone, with no LLM, already completes 0.917. In this environment each cause produces a distinctive discrete observation, so the procedure rather than the model does the work, and the models' measurable contribution is limited to waiting for confirming evidence before stopping. Our results therefore show that small local models should not own the investigation procedure, not that the controller makes them better investigators; whether a model adds value where observations are ambiguous and must be read from raw logs remains untested. We release all code, protocols, and episode journals at https://github.com/lzwhehe/HESP.
